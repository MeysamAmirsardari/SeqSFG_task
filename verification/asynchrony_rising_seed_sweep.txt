The worst pair at 4.17 of 7 is chance for this universe: eight 7-channel groups drawn from 23
channels share 7*7/23 = 2.1 on average, and the largest of the 28 pairs lands near 4. Those two
groups are never adjacent -- consecutive sharing is exactly 0 -- so no pitch is ever heard twice
at the element rate.

WHY absent_order 'redrawn'. A 'roving' absent interval must carry one time-aligned group per
element: that is what makes a group start just as often in both classes, and it is the whole
reason this contrast is envelope-matched where a plain cloud is not. But with the order held
fixed, all eight of those groups are the SAME shape -- under 'rising', eight identical chirps on
new pitches. Nothing returns, and yet there is plainly something to hear. Redrawing the order
costs nothing: a permutation does not change the multiset of onset times inside an element, so
the envelope, the per-channel counts and the count trace are matched to the last sample, while
the 'no' loses its repeating shape as well as its repeating pitches.

WHAT THIS CANNOT BE. A matched absent class cannot be silence-plus-cloud. One group starts per
element in both classes; the listener's job is to notice that in a 'yes' trial the same seven
pitches keep coming back. If a 'no' trial sounded like nothing at all, the difference would be
level, and a machine would read it -- which is exactly what happens with absent_class
'incoherent' (learnt d' +1.34 at 0 ms, +0.62 at 4 ms, +0.52 at 16 ms; see git history of this
file) and with 'plain' (+2.51 pooled, 89.5% correct).
